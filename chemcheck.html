ChemCheck is a simple educational tool that demonstrates how mathematical operations can be used to understand atomic structure, ions, electronegativity, chemical bonding, and gas laws. Through interactive calculators, a periodic table, and graphical gas-law visualizations, users can explore how mathematical relationships are applied in basic chemistry.

Addition • Subtraction • Multiplication • Division • Ratios • Differences • Direct and Inverse Relationships • Numerical Comparison • Graphical Analysis

About Us

This website was created as our final project for Mathematics in the Modern World. We developed this project to help people who are interested in science, as well as those who want to explore the field of science, understand basic scientific concepts and the mathematical computations connected to them. Through the interactive tools provided by ChemCheck, users can learn and practice basic chemistry concepts in a simple and accessible way.

Gas Laws

Use the sliders to explore gas-law conversions and see how volume, pressure, temperature, and gas amount are related.

Avogadro's Law

V₁ / n₁ = V₂ / n₂

Direct Relationship

At constant temperature and pressure, volume changes directly with the amount of gas. The calculator solves for the final volume.

10 L
2 mol
5 mol

Conversion Result

V₂ = 25.00 L
V₂ = V₁ × (n₂ / n₁)
V₂ = 10 × (5 / 2)
V₂ = 25.00 L

Graph: Volume (V) is plotted against Amount of Gas (n).

Boyle's Law

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Inverse Relationship

At constant temperature, pressure and volume have an inverse relationship. The calculator solves for the final volume.

1 atm
10 L
2 atm

Conversion Result

V₂ = 5.00 L
V₂ = (P₁ × V₁) / P₂
V₂ = (1 × 10) / 2
V₂ = 5.00 L

Graph: Pressure (P) is plotted against Volume (V), showing an inverse curve.

Charles' Law

V₁ / T₁ = V₂ / T₂

Direct Relationship

At constant pressure, volume changes directly with absolute temperature measured in Kelvin. The calculator solves for final volume.

10 L
300 K
450 K

Conversion Result

V₂ = 15.00 L
V₂ = V₁ × (T₂ / T₁)
V₂ = 10 × (450 / 300)
V₂ = 15.00 L

Graph: Volume (V) is plotted against Temperature (T) in Kelvin.
